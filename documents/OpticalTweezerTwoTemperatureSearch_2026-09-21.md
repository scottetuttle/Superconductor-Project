# Two-temperature optical vortex search

The search entry point is `python tools/optical_vortex_search.py --config tools/config/optical_vortex_two_temperature_search.json`. It builds `configs/simulations/nbn_optical_vortex_two_temperature.json` on the unperforated 100 nm NbN film, seeds one off-node vortex, and sweeps applied field, absorbed Gaussian laser power, and path speed. The optical path begins on the vortex, holds briefly, moves 8 nm, then holds again. Current injection is zero in this experiment so Lorentz drift cannot masquerade as optical following. Each run uses the separate electron/phonon temperatures and Heun TDGL integration.

The detector demands a persistent signed vortex track throughout transport, at least 2 nm of motion along the commanded path, a position correlation of at least 0.75, and an RMS beam-vortex lag below 8 nm. Electron temperature reaching `Tc` rejects a trial because an optically melted core would not demonstrate capture of an intact vortex. A candidate then faces three paired controls: no laser, a stationary illuminated spot, and the same path in reverse. The moving-beam displacement must exceed both stationary controls by at least 1 nm and reverse motion must follow the reverse path. These are operational evidence of tracking, not proof of a microscopic optical-tweezer force.

The companion `tools/config/optical_vortex_two_temperature_slow_search.json` tests 1–5 km/s over 4 nm, with a longer per-trial time budget and a 1 nm motion threshold. This is closer to the thermal-response speed scale discussed in the [original optical vortex manipulation experiment](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms12801), though that experiment used a much larger niobium film and micron-sized spot. It is correspondingly expensive; start with `python tools/optical_vortex_search.py --config tools/config/optical_vortex_two_temperature_slow_search.json --max-trials 1` and resume the result directory if desired.

Every trial writes diagnostics and vortex trajectories to a numbered output directory. A confirmed candidate is rerun with final film maps (`final_state.png`, `current_and_field.png`, and `electron_phonon_film.png`), time series, kymographs, vortex trajectories, beam-vortex distance, a GIF, a readable `report.md`, and a JSON summary in `successful_case/`. `summary.json` records the full search configuration, tested combinations, qualification reasons, and control outcomes. The sweep halts at the first confirmed candidate by default. `--max-trials N` runs a bounded smoke test. `--resume benchmark_results/<existing search directory>` continues the same configuration from its last completed trial.

Initial pilot runs on the older perforated 50 nm film were negative: the vortex drifted toward an edge transverse to the optical path, and some tracks vanished. This motivated the larger film without a hole. Seeding exactly on a grid node also made initial winding detection ambiguous; the new experiment seeds between nodes. The current laser heating, electron-phonon coupling, and pinning strength are illustrative rather than measured NbN calibrations. The simulator lacks stochastic thermal activation, quasiparticle transport, a calibrated nonlinear electron-phonon law, and experimental absorption data. The present 8 nm paths run over roughly 0.04–0.8 ps; slower, longer experiments may be required for vortex relaxation and will cost substantially more. A negative search therefore limits this parameterized model and time window, not the physical feasibility of optical manipulation.

Completed fast sweep: `benchmark_results/optical_vortex_two_temperature_search_6/summary.json` records 24 combinations and **zero** accepted captures. Eighteen cases kept the vortex at its initial plaquette despite beam travel; four crossed `Tc`; two very fast cases were undersampled and were labeled track loss by the pilot code. The runner now samples every step when transport lasts fewer than eight time steps, and labels insufficient sampling separately. The highest measured peak electron temperature was 15.565 K against `Tc=15.5 K`. No case exceeded zero measured plaquette displacement along the path. The slow-search profile is the next physically important test; it is not yet validated as a capture regime.

One 4.07 ps, 1 km/s pilot at 0.2 T and 0.5 µW absorbed power reached 15.251 K without crossing `Tc`, but the vortex stayed on the initial plaquette (`benchmark_results/optical_vortex_slow_pilot/`). Repeating the same protocol with static pinning disabled (`configs/simulations/nbn_optical_vortex_unpinned.json`, results in `benchmark_results/optical_vortex_unpinned_pilot/`) gave the same tracked position. The small configured pin alone therefore does not explain this negative result. The initial seeded vortex is not equilibrated in darkness before illumination, which may confound these brief runs; dark relaxation and a beam initially offset from the vortex are high-priority follow-ups. No experimental capture claim is supported yet.

## Force-focused rethink (2026-09-21)

The optical term in this code is only absorbed Gaussian **heat** deposited into the electron bath (`src/shs/optics/moving_laser.py`); it does not directly push a vortex. Temperature changes the local TDGL linear coefficient, while a static Gaussian defect reduces that coefficient. A thermal attraction is therefore only an emergent free-energy-gradient effect. The current outputs measure peak temperature and integer plaquette winding, but do not measure the electron-temperature gradient *at the vortex*, the local GL coefficient gradient, or subcell core displacement. They cannot yet distinguish a weak force from lattice locking or transient relaxation.

For the configured NbN example, the estimated electron diffusion time across the 10 nm spot is `Ce sigma^2 / ke = 14.3 ps`, the electron–phonon transfer time is `Ce/G = 100 ps`, and the normalized TDGL relaxation scale `u tau` is about `0.291 ps`. These are estimates from the configured constant coefficients, not calibrated NbN measurements. The completed fast paths last 0.04–0.8 ps; even the slow pilot lasts only 4.07 ps. The beam starts directly on the core, where its symmetric heating has zero local gradient initially, then moves only 0.4–0.8 Gaussian standard deviations. Thus the applied thermal drive may be both late and weakly directed in the short runs. The vortex detector reports only the center of a 1 nm plaquette; subcell motion is invisible.

The next **single controlled experiment** should dark-relax the seeded vortex at fixed field, then place a stationary beam 1–2 spot widths to one side while recording electron and phonon temperature maps, `|psi|`, phase, current, and a subcell vortex-center estimate. At every saved time, report the gradient of electron temperature and of the effective GL coefficient at the core, the local order-parameter suppression, maximum temperature relative to `Tc`, and motion relative to a no-laser control. Reverse the beam offset to test whether the response reverses. Only after a static offset causes a reproducible directed response should a slowly moving beam and velocity sweep resume. A dark-equilibrated state should be reused across powers and velocities to reduce cost.

The [single-vortex optical manipulation experiment](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms12801) used a continuous-wave 561 nm beam and attributes below-`Tc` dragging to laser-induced thermal gradients overcoming pinning. It estimates an upper drive speed around 1 km/s for its micron-scale spot and roughly 1 ns thermal response. That scale is not directly transferable to this 10 nm NbN spot, but it makes the present 10–200 km/s fast sweep a poor capture test. Optical carrier frequency is averaged out by the current heat model; adding a carrier-frequency parameter would not address the missing force diagnostic. Temporal power modulation may matter later if a nonthermal quasiparticle or resonant mechanism is identified and modeled.

`tools/optical_gradient_probe.py` implements the stationary-offset test using `tools/config/optical_gradient_probe.json`. It dark-prepares one seeded vortex, clones the same physical state into a dark control and equal positive/negative beam offsets, and records electron/phonon temperatures, temperature and effective-GL-coefficient gradients at the core, integer winding, a quadratic `|psi|` core estimate, and a second subcell estimate from the complex-`psi` zero. Its figures compare core gradient and movement and show final temperature/order-parameter maps. The gradients are **drive proxies**, not measured forces or a calibrated decomposition of vortex drag and pinning. The two subcell estimators can be biased differently by a broad hotspot; directional movement should agree between them and exceed dark drift before interpreting it as vortex transport.

The 0.5 µW, 10 nm sigma stationary probe at a 10 nm beam offset found a symmetric, dark-subtracted complex-zero shift of +0.543 nm toward the +y beam and -0.541 nm toward the -y beam after 2.5 ps (`benchmark_results/optical_gradient_probe_3/`). The corresponding electron gradients at the core were +0.0466 and -0.0465 K/nm; peak electron temperature was 14.842 K, below `Tc=15.5 K`, and one +1 vortex remained. Independent quadratic-amplitude fits also moved toward each beam by about 0.584 nm. This is **directional subcell motion in the configured model**, not yet a demonstrated full-cell or sustained moving-beam capture. The earlier plaquette-only trajectory CSVs were insensitive to this shift. Existing runs at the same power and duration suggest about 0.580 nm toward a 20 nm offset and 0.315 nm toward a 30 nm offset, but these are three isolated runs rather than a completed controlled parameter sweep.

The user-created `tools/config/optical_gradient_probe_sweep_example.json` is run with `python tools/optical_gradient_probe_sweep.py --config tools/config/optical_gradient_probe_sweep_example.json`. It averages the two mirrored, dark-subtracted, **projected** complex-zero shifts and writes a distance-versus-offset plot, per-case CSVs, a current best-case image set, and an incremental JSON summary. `--max-cases N` bounds a run; `--resume <result-directory>` continues with the same physical settings and step overrides. The example's five offsets, three powers, and 1,000 exposure steps imply roughly 31,100 coupled steps before thermal cutoffs, so short staged runs are appropriate before committing that time. The sweep rejects a claimed directional response if either branch crosses `Tc`, loses the signed vortex, or ends early. The fastest diagnostic path to moving-beam work is to confirm which **subcritical** offset/power pair gives a clear mirrored response, then test a beam that advances only after the vortex has shifted toward its initial offset.
