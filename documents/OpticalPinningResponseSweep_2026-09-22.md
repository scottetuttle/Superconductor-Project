# Optical pinning response sweep

Run `python tools/optical_pinning_response_sweep.py --config tools/config/optical_pinning_response_sweep.json`. A short paired check is `python tools/optical_pinning_response_sweep.py --max-cases 2 --dark-steps 40 --exposure-steps 80`. The config is the authority for pinning on/off, defect strength and size, beam distance, absorbed power, thermal/TDGL base simulation, and measurement cadence. Previous results are preserved in numbered output directories.

Each case seeds a +1 vortex, prepares it with the laser off, and clones that state into stationary-laser and dark branches. A zero pinning strength is an unpinned control. Positive strength activates the existing Gaussian reduction of the linear GL coefficient at the specified site. The default defect sits at the seeded vortex. It is a *phenomenological* pinning site, not a measured defect in NbN, and its strength is dimensionless. Preparation is repeated for each strength because the equilibrium vortex state depends on pinning.

`trajectories.csv` records the bilinear complex-psi zero, dark-subtracted displacement and sampled velocity toward the beam, electron temperature gradient at the vortex, pinning suppression and its GL-coefficient gradient, electron temperature, and vortex count. `sweep_summary.csv`, `summary.json`, and `response.png` compare cases. Temperature and pinning gradients are local drive **proxies**. They must not be called calibrated force components in N/m, and the finite-window velocity can be noisy at subcell resolution. A valid early response requires a unique vortex, temperatures below Tc, and completion of the requested window. Stronger pinning can make depinning slower or impossible within that window; absence of motion over a short run is not proof that optical motion is impossible.

The published [optical single-flux-quantum experiment](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms12801) used a subcritical thermal gradient to overcome vortex pinning. Its reported powers and gradient threshold belong to a 90 nm niobium film and micron-scale spot; this simulation uses a 2 nm NbN film and a 10 nm spot. Directly transferring those numerical thresholds would be unjustified. Quantitative comparison requires measured or independently justified NbN thermal conductivity, electron/phonon heat capacities and coupling, absorption and spot profile, vortex pinning energy landscape, and vortex mobility. A credible calibration sequence is: match stationary laser temperature profiles and cooling transients; match a measured zero-laser depinning threshold; then compare optical depinning probabilities and velocity versus local gradient. Only after those checks should a pulse-feedback controller use a target gradient.

## First paired result

`benchmark_results/optical_pinning_response_sweep_1` compared pinning strength 0 with 0.025 at 15 nm beam offset and 250 nW absorbed power, after 40 dark preparation steps and 80 exposure steps (0.8 ps). Both cases stayed below Tc and retained one vortex. Dark-subtracted toward-beam displacements were 0.022936 and 0.022861 nm, respectively; final electron gradients were both about 0.007329 K/nm. This small change is not evidence of a resolved depinning threshold. It indicates that this short run and weak illustrative defect do not yet separate pinned from unpinned response. Follow-up should lengthen the observation window only after measuring thermal buildup and select defect strengths by a separate zero-laser depinning or relaxation benchmark. The runner reuses dark preparation and dark exposure per strength across additional laser settings to reduce sweep cost.
