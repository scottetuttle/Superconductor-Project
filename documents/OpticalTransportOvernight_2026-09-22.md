# Overnight optical vortex transport

Run from the project root:

`python tools/optical_transport_overnight.py --config tools/config/optical_transport_overnight.json`

For a short check, use `python tools/optical_transport_overnight.py --dark-steps 2 --max-steps 5`. To resume an interrupted production run, supply its exact result directory, for example `python tools/optical_transport_overnight.py --config tools/config/optical_transport_overnight.json --resume benchmark_results/optical_transport_overnight`. The resume configuration must match the original. Output directories are numbered when the base name already exists; copy the actual directory printed by the runner.

The default attempts to move a single +1 vortex 5 nm toward +y on the 100 nm NbN film. The beam begins 15 nm ahead, uses 250 nW absorbed power, and follows the last accepted complex-order-parameter zero at a capped speed. Pinning is optional in the tool config and disabled in the initial overnight experiment. This isolates whether long exposure and thermal cycling can move the vortex before adding a depinning threshold. Enable the configured Gaussian defect for a later matched run. The controller is external; no laser force is inserted directly into TDGL.

Peak electron temperature controls a hysteretic laser switch: off at 15.0 K, back on after cooling to 14.6 K, with a 10-step minimum dwell. A 15.4 K hard limit stops the run before the 15.5 K material Tc. These are exploratory operating limits, not calibrated NbN optical thresholds. The run also stops at target arrival (within 0.25 nm), vortex loss or topology change, solver failure, 30,000 steps, ten wall-clock hours per invocation, or three consecutive 1,000-step windows with less than 0.05 nm of targetward progress each. Stall detection uses net motion and can end a run that is thermally cycling without useful transport.

`diagnostics.csv` is appended and flushed during the run. It includes vortex position, displacement toward target, sampled velocity, local electron and phonon temperature, peak electron temperature, local temperature and GL-coefficient gradients, laser state and commanded beam position, and vortex count. `state.json` points to an atomic `checkpoint_*.npz` containing the complete evolving fields and controller state. The runner checkpoints every 200 steps; on resume it discards CSV rows newer than the committed checkpoint to avoid double-counting. `run_status.json` reports the stop reason and last committed state. A final `transport_diagnostics.png` plots motion, temperature, and gradient. An interruption can lose at most the steps since the last checkpoint, not the whole run.

This is an experiment, not a guarantee of capture. The 250 nW source has shown small early subcell responses, but a few-nanometer relocation has not been demonstrated, and a 10-hour wall-time limit may end well before 30,000 physical steps on some machines. Subcell velocity is obtained from finite differences of interpolated vortex zeros and can be noisy. A moving thermal peak can lag the beam; an on/off thermal controller protects the temperature but does not by itself maximize the force at the vortex. If the run stalls, inspect gradient direction, temperature history, laser duty cycle, and targetward motion before changing power or pinning. Quantitative comparison to published niobium optical manipulation requires calibration of NbN thermal and pinning parameters.

## Retrospective visualization

Run `python tools/visualize_optical_transport.py benchmark_results/<run-directory>` to create `transport_trajectory.gif`, trajectory and thermal kymograph plots, and a checkpoint-field image with the recorded beam and vortex paths. The position-time plots are trajectory kymographs rather than spatial field kymographs. The overnight runner currently retains only its latest committed full-field checkpoint, so a completed run cannot be turned retrospectively into a time-resolved temperature, phase, or order-parameter movie. Future runs would need periodic visualization snapshots or a configurable retained-checkpoint history for that output.
